```
Create one vertical portrait card illustration (about 1:1.43 portrait - a 66 x 94 mm card that fits a 70 x 99 mm card sleeve) for an AR pet game. This is the "Divine" card of a four-card tarot-frog set. It will be printed matte and recognized by a phone camera from grayscale frames, so the artwork must keep a bold graphic silhouette and an even, all-over texture.

Style: flat woodcut 2D illustration in the spirit of Marseille tarot - large flat color fields, thick black outlines, crisp hard edges. No realistic shading, no gradients, no glow, no blur, no depth of field, no painterly rendering. Absurd cartoon proportions: big round head, chubby pear-shaped body. Retro screen-print texture crossed with modern flat design. Every large color field carries a subtle all-over texture of stippling, fine hatching, tiny stars or small leaves; the texture stays within plus/minus 5% brightness, varies in size, angle and density, never falls into a regular repeat, and no large blank patch is left untextured. Every edge is razor sharp - no haze, no film grain, no noise.

Subject - the character is a fixed design, copy it exactly: reproduce the original character shown in Image 2, a chubby toy-like 3D-rendered mascot nicknamed Naiwa. It is NOT a generic frog and NOT a realistic frog; do not redesign or "improve" it. One continuous bell-pear silhouette: a wide rounded dome head with no neck that merges directly into an oversized round body; the huge round belly is the widest point of the whole figure, tapering down into very short stubby arms and legs with no visible elbows or knees. Hands and feet are a contrasting pale tan / cream color, clearly different from the body hue - mitten-like paws with three to four rounded finger hints, and broad flat feet with three separate rounded toe lobes, no webbing. Two large dome-shaped eyes sit on the top-front of the head, very close together near the midline and protruding slightly above the head outline. The mouth is a small gentle closed smile - a short dark arc with both corners clearly turned up, fully closed, never a flat straight line - giving a calm good-natured look. The skin is matte clay-like with soft diffuse shading only - no wet specular highlights, no warts, no bumps. This variant's skin is light lavender purple; its belly is the large, almost circular cream-white patch with a crisp hard edge and a thin darker outline. This variant's eyes are two pure-white ovals with no pupil and no iris at all - no green iris, no black pupil. No eyebrows, eyelashes, blush, tail or markings; the only things the character wears are the papal vestments and the triple crown specified below.

Vestments - this card is the pope, so the frog wears full papal vestments fitted onto its round body: a long cream-white alb with a fine gold-trimmed collar, its front falling open so the big cream belly patch stays fully visible; a deep violet rounded cope draped over the shoulders and down the back and sides, clasped at the chest with a round gold morse, every edge trimmed in gold; and a narrow stole down the front carrying three small gold crosses that echo the triple cross. The hem flares gently but never wider than the round belly, which stays the widest point of the whole figure, and it stops above the feet so the short stubby legs and the broad three-toed feet stay visible. Every fold follows the frog's rounded silhouette - soft rounded drapes, no human shoulders, no neck hole, no angular vestment cuts. The mitten hands stay bare - no gloves. The alb is warm ivory white with gold trim; the cope is deep violet, darker than the skin, with gold trim.

Pose, from Image 1 (The Hierophant): the frog stands upright and facing front - standing, not seated. One hand is raised in a blessing gesture, approximated by a simple round mitten shape with no fingers; the other hand holds a triple-cross staff vertically. Its eyes are two pure-white ovals with no pupil and no iris, and its mouth is the same small gentle closed smile with corners slightly upturned. The overall bearing is solemn and majestic: upright, frontal, perfectly symmetrical, utterly still.

Symbols: a triple crown sitting on top of the head, leaving the face and both white oval eyes fully visible; two dark pillars with dense fine carving texture standing behind it; a pair of crossed keys lying on the ground at its feet; a step platform behind the frog, which stands on it with both feet fully visible, one step above the title band.

Hard constraints: all vestments are drawn directly onto the frog's body, with contact shading where cloth meets skin; do not draw floating, detached or standalone cloth pieces, and do not place any vestment anywhere except on the frog. The cope and the alb must not cover the face, the white oval eyes, the cream belly patch, the mitten hands or the three-toed feet.

Composition: the frog is centered and fills the middle of the card, standing tall and symmetrical behind the step platform. Fill the upper and side zones with the pillar shape, key shapes and fine texture - no untextured blank areas.

Frame: vertical; an outer band of fine low-contrast ornament; inside it a double-outlined tarot frame (thick outer line, thin inner line, small corner flourishes, total frame width under 3% of the card width); inside the frame sits the illustration; the bottom fifth of the card is a quiet, finely textured title band with no key elements, separated from the illustration by a thin horizontal rule or a branch; a small empty plaque at the top center. The frog's face and the key symbols stay out of the outer 8% border band.

Tonal design, and full color is mandatory: the background is a near-black deep-purple ground at roughly 8-14% brightness, far darker than the frog's body at 88-96%, a difference of at least 75 points. The belly is 15-25% brighter than the body; the ivory alb and the gold trim each sit at their own brightness step so the figure never flattens into one bright mass; eyes and limbs each sit at their own brightness step. The output must be a vivid, fully colored illustration - it must not be a black-and-white, grayscale, monochrome or line-sketch image, and the purple skin must stay clearly visible.

Text: render no text of any kind anywhere in the image - no letters, words, numbers, roman numerals, titles or watermarks. The title band and the top plaque stay completely empty.

References: Image 1 is the 1909 Rider-Waite tarot scan of The Hierophant - take from it the papal vestment forms (long alb, shoulder cope, front stole), the raised blessing gesture, the frontal upright pose and the crown-pillars-keys symbols, but redraw the vestments in this card's own palette (ivory, gold, deep violet) fitted to the frog's round body; do not copy its orange-red robe colors, its grey-white low-contrast pillars, its realistic line shading or its paper tone. Image 2 is our own 3D render of this exact character - copy the character's silhouette, proportions, colors, eye design and hand-and-foot design from it exactly; where any wording in this prompt conflicts with Image 2, Image 2 wins; never substitute a generic frog. Do not take its pose from Image 2. Image 3 is our approved classic card of the same series - match its visual style, frame design, texture density and rendering approach exactly; change only the frog's color, pose, symbols and background described above.

Glow rule: the eyes may carry at most a faint self-lit look; every other edge, including the crown, vestment trim, keys and staff, is a hard-edged flat shape - no glow, no bloom, no halo.
```
